# HiAir Website 2026 Audit

Date: 2026-08-31

Audit branch: `feat/hiair-premium-website-2026`

Production source baseline: `main` at `23eeea664d210dc7f539f2a301dc54042d64e02f`

Production domain: `https://hiair.io/`

## Executive finding

The current site is technically stronger than an early landing page: it has a verified App Store link, useful original SEO pages, legal pages, structured data, a sitemap, internal link validation, privacy-aware analytics, and a guarded Cloudflare deployment. Its main weakness is product presentation. The first screen uses a hand-built illustrative dashboard instead of a real shipped product screenshot, the page relies on many visually repetitive cards, the headline takes too long to communicate the core job, and some marketing claims are broader than current implementation evidence.

The redesign must preserve the existing static architecture, store-link source of truth, generated SEO pages, validation scripts, Growth OS telemetry, and production deployment workflow while replacing the homepage's simulated-product-first presentation with real product evidence and clearly labeled examples.

## Production and store truth

- The official App Store page resolves for app id `6773610034`, shows HiAir version 1.1, iPhone/iPad support, free download with in-app purchases, and no public ratings summary.
- `web/config/store-links.json` records iOS as `PUBLIC_CONFIRMED` and Android as `NOT_PUBLIC`.
- The public Google Play details URL for package `com.hiair` is intentionally absent from site configuration.
- Android code and publishing automation exist, but public Google Play availability is not proven. No Google Play CTA may be shown.
- App Store copy markets live air, heat, conditional pollen/smoke coverage, best-time windows, Apple Health, travel, family risk, work-site heat, and Premium. Implementation evidence narrows some of those claims, as recorded below.

## Stale or misleading content

1. The homepage hero is an illustrated CSS/product composite. It is labeled `Preview`, but its visual prominence can still be mistaken for shipped UI. A real captured iOS dashboard should replace it.
2. The site says Apple Health and Health Connect access “is available.” Apple Health is defensible for the public iOS product, but Health Connect belongs to an Android product that is not publicly listed. Homepage copy should focus on Apple Health.
3. The site lists pollen among combined triggers without consistently carrying the “when available” constraint. In this tree pollen is unavailable in the hazard engine.
4. The site lists smoke in several product explanations without consistently carrying an availability qualifier. In this tree smoke is unavailable in the hazard engine.
5. The Android waitlist is legitimate, but it competes visually with the primary App Store conversion. It should become a low-emphasis status note rather than the final dominant form.
6. “Safe windows” appears in repository/app copy. Marketing should prefer “more suitable outdoor windows” because HiAir is a wellness product and input coverage may be incomplete.
7. The current homepage has no truthful Free versus Premium explanation even though Premium is live in the App Store.
8. The current navigation reflects older SEO audience pages rather than the primary product journey.

Repository-wide search found historical beta, TestFlight, roadmap, and coming-soon references in engineering/release documentation. Those documents are evidence records and should not be globally rewritten. Public web content must not expose obsolete launch-state language. The current public website already avoids “coming to iOS” and a fake Play button.

## Visual audit

### Strengths to preserve

- Distinct HiAir identity, consistent dark/cyan/violet palette, and real brand assets.
- Strong first-screen App Store visibility.
- Generous desktop spacing and readable primary body text.
- Existing reduced-motion handling and keyboard-operable navigation/FAQ.
- The product value is already framed as guidance rather than raw weather.

### Weaknesses to fix

- The current H1 (“Clearer heat and air decisions for your day”) is accurate but less immediate than “Know the best time to go outside.”
- The hero shows an attractive constructed mockup rather than shipped UI.
- Many sections use identical small cards; the page reads as a feature catalogue rather than four memorable product stories.
- Emoji icons weaken the premium consumer-product feel and create inconsistent platform rendering.
- The gradient/glass treatment is applied nearly everywhere, reducing contrast between important and secondary content.
- The long homepage exceeds 8,000 CSS pixels and repeats similar explanations before reaching pricing, science, and FAQ.
- Product differentiation from a normal weather/AQI app is described, but not demonstrated in one concentrated visual comparison.

## Conversion audit

- Primary App Store CTA exists in header, hero, final section, FAQ, and footer; this is a strong base.
- CTA language is inconsistent between text buttons and official badge; the redesign should preserve one primary action and use the badge where store identity matters.
- No real-product proof is visible; this weakens trust despite the app being publicly shipped.
- No Premium value explanation exists.
- The Android email form adds cognitive load at the main conversion point.
- There is no lightweight interactive product experience to help a visitor understand the planner before leaving for the store.

## SEO audit

### Strengths

- Unique titles/descriptions, canonical URLs, indexable robots policy, a canonical sitemap, original guide pages, internal links, and `BreadcrumbList` on generated subpages.
- Homepage has `MobileApplication`, `Organization`, `WebSite`, and content-matching `FAQPage` JSON-LD.
- CI validates sitemap membership, canonical origin, metadata, internal links, 404 handling, store-link truth, and generator determinism.
- No meta-keywords anti-pattern.

### Risks and opportunities

- Open Graph uses the square app icon rather than a purpose-built social preview. A new asset should only be added if it can be produced and verified without inventing UI.
- The schema type should be normalized to `SoftwareApplication` with `applicationSubCategory`/offers only when factual. Existing `MobileApplication` is valid but narrower.
- `methodology/` has the right substance for Science & Data but the label is less discoverable in the primary journey. Improve the page and label rather than create a duplicative thin route.
- A homepage title/description should focus more strongly on best-time outdoor planning while retaining heat and air-quality relevance.
- Existing audience/guide pages are useful and should not be replaced with thin keyword routes.

## Accessibility audit

- Existing positives: skip link, semantic regions/headings, labeled navigation, explicit form labels, live form status, FAQ buttons with `aria-expanded`, escape-to-close mobile nav, and reduced-motion checks.
- The current decorative emoji icons are announced inconsistently across platforms despite `aria-hidden` wrappers.
- Several muted gray text styles on dark translucent surfaces appear close to minimum contrast and require automated/visual testing.
- The hero CSS mockup has a very long image alternative that mixes description and sample data; a real image should use concise alt text and adjacent visible explanation.
- Focus visibility is present in parts of the stylesheet but must be confirmed for every new interactive control.
- Screenshot review cannot prove WCAG compliance; keyboard, semantic, and automated checks are required after implementation.

## Performance audit

- Static HTML and vanilla JS are excellent foundations; there is no framework hydration cost.
- Google Fonts adds an external dependency and can delay text. The redesign should keep a robust system fallback and avoid adding more font families/weights.
- The animated SVG turbulence/filter and glow effects can be expensive on mobile GPUs.
- The CSS-only hero preview creates substantial paint complexity.
- Product screenshots are large source PNGs and must be copied selectively, dimensioned, compressed where practical, and lazy-loaded outside the hero.
- No baseline Lighthouse report is committed for the current production page. Measured results must be recorded after local/preview verification, not inferred.

## Content that cannot be claimed without more evidence

- A guaranteed safe time or medical-grade recommendation.
- Always-available pollen or smoke data.
- Fully wired mobile push notifications.
- Public Android availability.
- Any ratings score, review quote, user/download count, or “featured by” claim.
- Physical-device HealthKit E2E certification.
- Region-independent Premium prices.
- A broad travel mode beyond saved-place planning.

## Audit outcome

No P0 issue prevents redesign implementation. The highest product-marketing risks are the synthetic hero, unqualified pollen/smoke language, overbroad wearables wording, weak Premium explanation, and visually repetitive structure. These are addressed before and during the homepage refactor.
